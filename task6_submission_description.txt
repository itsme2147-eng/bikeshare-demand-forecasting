This is my final data science capstone: day-ahead hourly demand forecasting for a city bike-share system, built on 17,379 real hourly records from Washington DC's Capital Bikeshare system (2011 to 2012, UCI Machine Learning Repository). The report defines the problem, objectives, scope, requirements and success criteria before any modelling, then documents data collection with a verified download of two identical copies, a data audit, cleaning, feature engineering, exploratory analysis, modelling, evaluation, interpretation, recommendations, limitations and future scope.

The audit found that the file never records hours with zero rentals, plus sensor glitches in humidity, wind and feels-like temperature, and these drove documented cleaning decisions. I built leakage-safe features (history used only up to 24 hours before the target) and unit-tested them, with 22 passing tests. Models were validated chronologically: three naive baselines, a negative-binomial GLM, a random forest and LightGBM, trained before July 2012, tuned on July to September, and scored once on October to December 2012. LightGBM reached a WAPE of 17.9 percent against 28.8 percent for the best baseline, a 37.8 percent reduction, confirmed with Diebold-Mariano tests. SHAP and GLM rate ratios show demand is mostly persistence plus weather, with rain cutting demand by about 42 percent.

I also report what did not go perfectly: my pre-declared champion rule picked the random forest in a statistical tie and missed one success criterion, raw prediction intervals under-covered at 68.8 percent until conformal recalibration raised coverage to 81.4 percent, and the models fail on Hurricane Sandy and special days. The project includes a notebook pipeline, quantile forecasts with planning trade-offs, operational recommendations, a presentation and a GitHub repository.
